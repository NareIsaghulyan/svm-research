# Entry 001

## 1. Research Objective

The objective of this initial stage is to establish a foundational understanding of Support Vector Machines (SVM), investigate their mathematical principles, and explore the factors that influence their classification performance, robustness, and computational efficiency.

## 2. Current Knowledge

My experience with Machine Learning is relatively recent, having spent approximately two months exploring the field.
My current understanding of Support Vector Machines is introductory. I know that SVM is a supervised machine learning algorithm primarily used for classification, but also applicable to regression problems.
I understand that it is used to identify decision boundaries between classes, but I have not yet studied its mathematical foundations, optimization principles, or different kernel functions in depth.

## 3. Research Motivation

My goal is to investigate the full potential and limitations of Support Vector Machines rather than simply understand how to apply the algorithm.
I am particularly interested in the mathematical foundations behind SVM, the optimization problems it solves, and the theoretical principles that explain its classification behavior.
I also want to understand how SVM behaves under challenging conditions, including uneven class distributions, noisy training samples, and the presence of outliers.

## 4. Intended Investigation

The research will focus on the following areas:
Mathematical foundations: Investigating the geometric and mathematical principles behind SVM, including maximum-margin classification, optimization, support vectors, and kernel methods.
Kernel functions: Studying how different kernel functions influence decision boundaries and classification performance.
Class imbalance: Investigating how uneven class distributions affect classification performance and whether different approaches can improve the results.
Noise and outliers: Examining how SVM responds to noisy observations and outliers, and how regularization influences its robustness.
Generalization: Studying the relationship between model complexity, regularization, and performance on unseen data.
Computational efficiency: Investigating training time, inference latency, memory requirements, and the influence of dataset size and dimensionality.
Comparative investigation: Comparing SVM with other supervised learning algorithms under consistent experimental conditions.

## 5. Initial Research Questions

What mathematical principles make maximum-margin classification effective?
How do kernel functions influence the decision boundary?
How does the regularization parameter affect overfitting and generalization?
How sensitive is SVM to class imbalance, noise, and outliers?
Under what conditions does SVM perform better or worse than alternative classifiers?
What are the computational limitations of SVM?

## 6. Expected Research Outcomes

I aim to develop a rigorous understanding of SVM, supported by mathematical derivations, scientific literature, and reproducible experiments.
The intended outcome is an experimental evaluation of the algorithm's capabilities and limitations, accompanied by an analysis of the conditions under which different SVM configurations are effective.
I also intend to develop a reproducible implementation and document the findings in a research paper and GitHub repository.
